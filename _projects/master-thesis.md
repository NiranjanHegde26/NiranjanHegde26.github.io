I recently completed my thesis on bilingual language processing titled **Individual Differences of Inhibition Control in Bilingual Sentence Processing A Self-Paced Reading Approach**, focusing on how people manage words that exist in two languages but carry different meanings (known as interlingual homographs). Specifically, I examined how native German speakers who are fluent in English process such words while reading English sentences. This study was motivated from my project from coursework [Experimental Methods of Psycholinguistic Research](/projects/false-friends).

The study was based on the idea that bilinguals activate both of their languages in parallel. This raises an important question: does the presence of a cross-language word create interference during reading, and does a person’s inhibitory control ability help manage that interference?

To investigate this, participants completed a self-paced reading task in which they read sentences word by word. This was followed by cognitive tasks measuring inhibitory control, a language background questionnaire, and an English proficiency test. Reading times were analyzed at key points in the sentences where cross-language effects were most likely to appear.

Contrary to expectations, the results did not provide strong evidence that interlingual homographs caused measurable processing delays. While inhibitory control ability predicted overall reading speed, it did not specifically influence how participants processed cross-language words. Effects of English proficiency were present but limited and dependent on context.

These findings suggest that bilingual readers may manage cross-linguistic competition efficiently during second-language sentence comprehension, possibly without relying heavily on inhibitory control mechanisms. Alternatively, such effects may be subtle and require more sensitive experimental methods to detect. Overall, the study highlights the need for further research using complementary approaches to better understand inhibition in bilingual language processing.

This project is part of the **NLP for Receptive Multilingualism** (Blocked Software Project) offered by Saarland University for the Winter Semester of 2024/25 taught by [Dr. Wei Xue](https://wei3xue1.github.io/) and [Iuliia Zaitova](https://iuliiazaitova.github.io/). Receptive Multilingualsim is a cool term for a language mode that we often encounter in real world. We find individuals who can understand a language but cannot speak it. Similarly, there are cases where speakers of language X can easily comprehend language Y without any formal instruction, while native speakers of Y struggle to learn language X. I have personally heard stories (and some live interactions) between Tamil and Malayalam speakers (Two Dravidian languages). 

In this course, we worked on mutual intelligibility and receptive multilingualism in Slavic language family in translation tasks, focusing on Russian-speaking participants by analysing the experimental data. We had the experimental data from Russian native speakers translating phrases (freely or in MCQ tasks) from Slavic languages like Czech, Polish, Belarusian and Bulgarian. Our hypothesis was that Russian native speakers who had Ukrainian knowledge are better at translation task because Ukrainian facilitates to a certain extent for comprehension due to its similarity with abovementioned Slavic languages. 

We used NLP techniques and metrics such as Jaccard similarity and Levenshtein distance on translation correctness in free translation task and multiple-choice translation task (MCQ). Further, we used LLM-obtained metrics such as surprisal, [COMET](https://github.com/Unbabel/COMET) and [BERTScore](https://github.com/Tiiiger/bert_score) of translation quality as an outcome of the above factors to further understand the interplay between comprehension, translation and translation quality.

The results are pretty interesting and the abstract of this work has been accepted for presentationas a poster titled **"Does Ukrainian serve as a good bridge language for Russian speakers in understanding other Slavic languages?"** at the 9th Workshop on Multilingual Acqusition, Processing and Use happening in Madrid on Oct 3 and 4, 2025. In the meantime, we are preparing to submit our work to a journal for official publication.

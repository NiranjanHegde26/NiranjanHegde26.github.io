This study was conducted in collaboration with **Dr. Torsten Jachmann, Dr. Masato Nakamura**, and students from the **Experimental Methods of Psycholinguistic Research** course at **Saarland University (Summer 2024)**.

This study explores how German-English bilinguals process language, focusing on the influence of **false friends**—words that look similar in both languages but have different meanings. Results show that **false friends slow down reading times**, regardless of English proficiency, confirming **nonselective lexical access**, where both languages are activated simultaneously. However, more proficient speakers are better at distinguishing meanings, leading to higher comprehension accuracy. These findings support the **BIA+ model**, highlighting how bilinguals manage cross-language interference.

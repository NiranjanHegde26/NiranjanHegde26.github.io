This term paper titled **Exploring Lifelong Language Learning: From Human Brain to Artificial Intelligent Models** was part of the **NLP For Psycholinguistic Research** seminar offered by Saarland University for the Winter Semester of 2023/24 taught by [Dr. Pablo Contreras Kallens](https://www.contreraskallens.com/).

I wrote about the theories and studies that try to understand the human competence to remember (or forget) various aspects of language learning for a longer period, and the factors that affect the memory and forgetting. Later, drawing parallels to humans, I covered ideas from characteristics of artificial neural networks in terms of their memory retention and the efforts to avoid catastrophic forgetting (a phenomenon where the previously learnt knowledge is precipitously forgotten or erased with the learning of new knowledge).

This paper was my first attempt at writing a research-review-style report and as expected, I was sloppy. In spite of this, I felt that ideas presented with regards to language learning and memory are fascinating. I spent a lot of time reading about sleep and memory, and about the advantages of sleep in the learning process and memory. This is certainly a domain that is worth exploring further, and I am certainly willing to pick such works in future.
